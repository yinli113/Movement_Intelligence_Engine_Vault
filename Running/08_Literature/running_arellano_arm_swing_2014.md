# Arm swing restriction: experimental evidence

## Citation and verification
Arellano & Kram (2014), Journal of Experimental Biology 217:2456–2461. [DOI 10.1242/jeb.100420](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.100420). Author-hosted [full text, Results and Methods](https://grants.hhp.uh.edu/arellano/docs/J%20Exp%20Biol%202014%20Arellano.pdf) checked 2026-10-16.

## Experimental findings — Level 3
Thirteen participants ran normally and with three arm restrictions. Relative metabolic cost increased under restriction. Peak-to-peak **pelvis** axial rotation increased 63%, 102% and 101% in BACK, CHEST and HEAD conditions. Shoulder increases were 10%, 44% and 8%; the last was not statistically significant. These are condition effects, not individual cutoffs.

## Interpretation, not direct measurement
Rotational counterbalancing is a proposed explanation. The experiment does not establish individual muscle work, fascial recoil, head stabilization, or that naturally compact arms are dysfunctional. It does not prescribe an optimal arm amplitude.

## Correction record
Previous vault text swapped pelvis and shoulder percentages, stated n=10, and asserted extra torso work and head damping as measured findings. Those statements are superseded.
