# Literature - Running economy and lower-limb anthropometry in adult male Kenyan and Danish middle- and long-distance runners

## Citation

Larsen et al. (published online 2025; corrected/final 2026). *Running economy and lower-limb anthropometry in adult male Kenyan and Danish middle- and long-distance runners and in untrained adolescents*. Experimental Physiology. DOI: [10.1113/EP092758](https://doi.org/10.1113/EP092758).

## Topic

Anthropometric traits (lower limb segment mass, calf circumference) and their correlation with running economy across selected demographic cohorts.

## Principal Relevant Findings

1. **Distal limb mass and swing inertia**:
   - The study found an association between lower-leg cross-sectional area and oxygen cost. Swing inertia and internal mechanical work are possible explanations, not directly demonstrated outcomes.
2. **Sample-based observations, not racial universals**:
   - Differences observed between selected Kenyan and Danish runner cohorts are sample-based anthropometric distributions. They cannot be generalized into ethnic stereotypes or used to judge an individual athlete's running form.

## Limitations and Evidence Boundaries

- Observational cross-sectional study; anthropometry does not determine running form or tactical success in isolation.
- 2D video analysis cannot determine tissue composition, tendon morphology, or muscle mass distribution.
