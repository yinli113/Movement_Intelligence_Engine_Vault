# Literature - Structural and Potentially Modifiable Anthropometric Traits in Professional Kenyan and European Long-Distance Runners

## Citation

Spataro, F., et al. (2026). *Structural and Potentially Modifiable Anthropometric Traits in Professional Kenyan and European Long-Distance Runners: A Cross-Sectional Study*. Journal of Functional Morphology and Kinesiology, 11(3), 292. DOI: [10.3390/jfmk11030292](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk11030292).

Sample: 32 Kenyan vs. 20 European male professional marathon runners.

## Topic

Anthropometric phenotypes, limb proportions, body composition, and their relationship to distance running performance.

## Principal Relevant Findings

1. **Cohort-specific morphological profiles**:
   - Examined specific structural traits (e.g. crural index, pelvic dimensions, torso-to-leg ratios) in elite runners.
   - Reports sample-level anthropometric differences; adaptation of biomechanical coordination was not directly measured.
2. **Scientific boundary**:
   - Findings represent sample-level statistical differences, not universal biomechanical laws.

## Limitations and Evidence Boundaries

- Small elite cross-sectional sample; cannot be extrapolated as diagnostic of performance for recreational or diverse runners.
- Video kinematics cannot measure skeletal morphology or body composition directly.
