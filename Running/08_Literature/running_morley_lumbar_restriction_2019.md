# Literature - The effects of dorso-lumbar motion restriction on energy use and center of mass movement during running

## Citation

Morley, J. J., & Traum, E. (2019). *The effects of dorso-lumbar motion restriction on energy use and center of mass movement during running*. Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies, 23(3), 564–570. DOI: [10.1016/j.jbmt.2019.01.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2019.01.003).

## Topic

Spinal kinematics, experimental restriction of spinal movement, metabolic cost, and center of mass (CoM) excursion during running.

## Principal Relevant Findings

1. **Spinal motion is metabolically functional**:
   - Deliberately bracing or restricting dorso-lumbar spinal motion during running significantly increases oxygen consumption (raising metabolic energy cost).
   - Natural spinal movement (sagittal flexion-extension, lateral bending, and axial rotation) facilitates efficient energy transfer and smooth CoM progression.
2. **Impact on Center of Mass**:
   - Restricting spinal mobility forces compensatory alterations in lower limb mechanics and increases vertical/lateral CoM acceleration.
   - Preserving natural torso mobility is essential for locomotor economy; rigid "upright and frozen" torso postures are biomechanically counterproductive.

## Limitations and Evidence Boundaries

- Experimental external brace model; does not prove that every natural spinal posture is automatically optimal.
- Video analysis cannot quantify oxygen consumption or metabolic cost in Joules.
