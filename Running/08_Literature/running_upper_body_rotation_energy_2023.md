# Literature - Relationship between Longitudinal Upper Body Rotation and Energy Cost of Running in Junior Elite Long-Distance Runners

## Citation

(2023). *Relationship between Longitudinal Upper Body Rotation and Energy Cost of Running in Junior Elite Long-Distance Runners*. Sports, 11(10), 204. PMID: [37888531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888531/). DOI: [10.3390/sports11100204](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports11100204). PMC: [PMC10611096](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10611096/).

Study design: Observational cross-sectional study in n=86 junior elite long-distance runners using wearable inertial sensors, respiratory gas analysis and blood sampling.

## Topic

Longitudinal (axial) upper body rotation, shoulder and pelvic excursion, and the energy cost of running (running economy).

## Principal Relevant Findings

1. **Upper-body rotation associates with lower energy cost**:
   - In 86 junior elite runners, upper-trunk rotation had a slight negative correlation with energy cost of running (better running economy).
   - This directly challenges the conventional coaching belief that any visible upper-body rotation is wasted motion that degrades economy.
2. **Speed-dependent increase in rotation**:
   - Both shoulder and pelvic longitudinal rotation increased significantly as running speed increased.
   - Elastic recoil and momentum mechanisms are interpretations; this association does not establish either mechanism.

## Limitations and Evidence Boundaries

- Observational cross-sectional association; does not prove that deliberately forcing greater upper body rotation will improve an individual runner's economy.
- Evaluated junior elite runners; mechanics may differ in recreational, master, or injured runners.
- Video 2D line tilt cannot directly measure longitudinal transverse rotation or caloric expenditure.

## Application Implication

Supports TillYes philosophy: an unusual or prominent upper-body rotation pattern is not automatically inefficient or a flaw requiring correction. Guardrails prohibit flagging upper body rotational excursion as an impairment without specific clinical or kinetic deficits.
