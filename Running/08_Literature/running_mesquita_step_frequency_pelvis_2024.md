# Step frequency and pelvis–thigh coordination
Mesquita RM, Morin J-B, Dewolf AH. Online 2024; print 2025, J Appl Biomech 41(1):8–17. [DOI 10.1123/jab.2024-0016](https://doi.org/10.1123/jab.2024-0016); [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39577406/), checked 2026-10-16.

## Experimental findings — Level 3
Eight recreational male runners varied speed and step frequency. Lower frequencies increased frontal pelvic roll; thigh abduction responses differed between participants. Sagittal hip ROM increased with step frequency; pelvic motion contributed differently at lower frequency. Coordination was assessed with modified vector coding.

## Corrections and limits
The previous claim that lower cadence uniformly increases thigh ROM was unsupported. The gravity/longer-stance explanation and a side-specific cadence rule were not demonstrated. Do not infer cadence from pelvic excursion or equate cadence with a single left/right stance duration. Full methods and numerical effect sizes remain to be reviewed; no intervention prescription or injury causation follows.
