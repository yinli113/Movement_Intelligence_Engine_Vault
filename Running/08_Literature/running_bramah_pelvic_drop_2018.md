# Literature - Is There a Pathological Gait Associated with Common Soft Tissue Running Injuries?

## Citation

Bramah C, Preece SJ, Gill N, Herrington L. (2018). *Is there a pathological gait associated with common soft tissue running injuries? A systematic review of 3D motion capture studies.* British Journal of Sports Medicine, 52(14), 897-904. PMID: 29982189. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29982189/

## Topic

Contralateral pelvic drop, coronal kinematics, and soft-tissue injury risk in recreational runners.

## Principal Relevant Findings

1. **Increased Odds of Injury per Degree of Pelvic Drop**: For every 1° increase in contralateral pelvic drop during stance, there is an 80% increased odds of a recreational runner being classified as injured (OR = 1.80, 95% CI 1.08 to 3.00).
2. **Clinical Benchmarks**: Non-injured healthy runners typically maintain dynamic coronal pelvic tilt excursion within approximately 3° to 5°.
3. **Common Pathologies**: Elevated pelvic drop is strongly associated with patellofemoral pain syndrome (PFPS), iliotibial band syndrome (ITBS), and tibial stress injuries due to compensatory hip adduction and internal rotation.

## Limitation

Statistical associations in 3D laboratory motion capture do not prove retrospective causation from 2D monocular video alone. Elevated drop must remain an observable descriptor and Level D hypothesis requiring clinical correlation, rather than an automated medical diagnosis.
