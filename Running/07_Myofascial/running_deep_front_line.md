# Running - Deep Front Line (Inner System)

## Definition

A Level D myofascial hypothesis node representing the deep longitudinal axial core that provides sagittal and coronal structural centration, arch support, and pelvic levelness during single-leg stance in running.

## Role in Running Locomotion

1. **Single-Leg Structural Plumb-Line Pillar**:
   - During single-limb support, the Deep Front Line acts as the vertical axial core, anchoring the body from the medial foot arch up to the cranium.
   - Resists coronal collapse (excessive pelvic drop) and sagittal collapse during midstance compression.
2. **Pelvic Levelness and Axial Centration**:
   - Mediates the vertical equilibrium of the pelvis on the femoral head via the coordinated tone of [[psoas_major]], [[adductor_magnus]], and the pelvic floor.
3. **Deep Plantar Support**:
   - The deep calf compartment and [[tibialis_posterior]] provide dynamic arch support and deceleration of pronation at initial contact.

## Kinematic Observables and Measurable Proxies

- Pelvic-center vertical and lateral displacement (`running.body_centre.proxy`).
- Midstance continuous posture alignment: Single continuous forward posture line from stance foot through knee, pelvis, spine, and head ([[gruenke_kiptum_running_analysis_2023]]).
- Ankle-knee landing offset and projected shank inclination.

## Asymmetry Hypothesis Guidance

- If a runner demonstrates uncompensated single-leg pelvic drop, medial knee collapse, or excessive lateral pelvic shift without coordinated trunk counter-rotation:
  - Investigate the Inner System (Deep Front Line) axial centration capacity.
  - Testable hypothesis: Asymmetric single-leg pelvic drop may reflect altered deep arch support ([[tibialis_posterior]]) or deep hip/psoas stabilization rather than superficial gluteal fatigue alone.

## Limitations and Evidence Boundaries

- The Deep Front Line is an internal anatomical continuity; camera video detects only external surface landmark proxies.
- Kinematic video analysis **cannot** evaluate deep psoas activation, diaphragm excursion, or fascial stiffness.
- Never diagnose "core weakness" or "psoas inhibition" from video alone.

## Reporting Language

- *Permitted*: "Observed single-leg pelvic alignment differences may support an Inner System (Deep Front Line) axial centration hypothesis, which may warrant clinical correlation."
- *Forbidden*: "Deep front line core instability diagnosed."
- *Forbidden*: "Psoas is turned off."
