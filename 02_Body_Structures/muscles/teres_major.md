# Teres Major

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Teres Major** is a thick muscle of the shoulder girdle that runs alongside the lower border of [[latissimus_dorsi]]. Known functionally as the "lats little helper", it acts directly on the glenohumeral joint to extend, adduct, and internally rotate the arm.

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin (Proximal Attachments)**:
  - Oval area on the dorsal surface of the inferior angle of the scapula.
  - Fibrous septa separating it from [[infraspinatus]] and [[teres_minor]].
- **Insertion (Distal Attachments)**:
  - Medial lip of the intertubercular (bicipital) groove of the anterior shaft of the humerus.

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[shoulder_joint]] (glenohumeral joint).
- **Primary Actions**:
  - [[shoulder_internal_rotation]]
  - [[shoulder_adduction]]
  - Glenohumeral extension from a flexed position.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[superficial_front_arm_line]] (SFAL) / [[functional_lines]]**: Integrates closely with the humeral attachment of [[latissimus_dorsi]], transmitting adduction and rotational power from the torso to the upper limb.
