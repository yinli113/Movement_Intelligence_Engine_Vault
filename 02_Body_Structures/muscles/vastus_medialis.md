# Vastus Medialis

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Vastus Medialis** is the teardrop-shaped medial component of the quadriceps femoris muscle group. Its distal horizontal/oblique fibers (frequently designated as the *Vastus Medialis Oblique* or VMO) are essential for tracking the patella medially within the femoral trochlear groove to counteract the lateral pull of the [[vastus_lateralis]].

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin (Proximal Attachments)**:
  - Inferior end of the intertrochanteric line of the femur.
  - Pectineal line and medial lip of the linea aspera.
  - Proximal portion of the medial supracondylar line.
  - Medial intermuscular septum (adjacent to [[adductor_magnus]] and [[adductor_longus]]).
- **Insertion (Distal Attachments)**:
  - Medial margin and base of the patella.
  - Tibial tuberosity via the common quadriceps tendon and infrapatellar ligament.
  - Medial tibial plateau/condyle via expansions of the medial patellar retinaculum.

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[knee_joint]] (monoarticular).
- **Primary Actions**:
  - [[knee_extension]]: Powers knee extension throughout range, providing terminal extension lock-out.
  - **Patellar Medial Stabilization**: Balances lateral vector forces to protect against patellofemoral subluxation and tracking dysfunction.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[superficial_front_line]] (SFL)**: Connects into the anterior knee extensor complex and prepatellar fascia.
- **[[deep_front_line]] (DFL) Proximity**: Its proximal fascial boundary is continuous with the medial intermuscular septum and adductor canal fascia.
