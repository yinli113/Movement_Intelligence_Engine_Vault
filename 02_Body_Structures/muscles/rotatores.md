# Rotatores

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Rotatores** are small, highly innervated paraspinal muscles lying beneath the [[multifidus]]. While their mechanical torque is modest, their extremely high muscle spindle density makes them key proprioceptors of segmental spinal orientation and rotation.

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin**:
  - Transverse processes of vertebrae (most prominent in thoracic spine).
- **Insertion**:
  - Lamina and spinous process of adjacent vertebra (rotatores brevis) or 2 levels above (rotatores longus).

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[thoracic_spine]], [[lumbar_spine]], [[cervical_spine]].
- **Primary Actions**: Segmental proprioception, contralateral [[thoracic_rotation]], and intervertebral stabilization.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[deep_front_line]] (DFL) & [[spiral_line]]**: Deepest rotational sensor track along the axial column.
