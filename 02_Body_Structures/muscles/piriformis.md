# Piriformis

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Piriformis** is a flat, pyramidal muscle of the deep gluteal region. Passing through the greater sciatic foramen, it links the axial spine/pelvis ([[sacrum]]) directly to the appendicular skeleton (greater trochanter of the femur). It exhibits functional mechanical inversion based on hip flexion angle.

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin (Proximal Attachments)**:
  - Anterior surface of the sacrum between the S2, S3, and S4 anterior sacral foramina.
  - Margin of the greater sciatic notch of the ilium.
  - Deep fascial fibers from the [[sacrotuberous_ligament]].
- **Insertion (Distal Attachments)**:
  - Superior border and medial aspect of the greater trochanter of the femur.

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[hip_joint]], sacroiliac joint / [[sacrum]].
- **Kinematic Inversion**:
  - **In Hip Extension (<60 deg Flexion)**: Primary [[hip_external_rotation]], [[hip_abduction]], and dynamic stabilization of the femoral head in the acetabulum.
  - **In Deep Hip Flexion (>60-90 deg Flexion)**: Its line of pull shifts anterior to the hip joint axis of rotation, converting the muscle into a **hip internal rotator** and horizontal abductor.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[deep_front_line]] (DFL)**: Connects deep pelvic wall structures to the posterior hip rotator cuff.
- **[[superficial_back_line]] (SBL) / [[sacrotuberous_ligament]] Connection**: Bridges tension across the sacral base and sacrotuberous ligament.
