# Obturator Internus

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Obturator Internus** is a deep posterior hip rotator that originates inside the true pelvis, passes through the lesser sciatic notch, and inserts onto the greater trochanter. In Anatomy Trains, it is a key pelvic outlet station of the [[deep_front_line]] (DFL).

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin**:
  - Inner surface of the anterolateral pelvic wall and obturator membrane.
- **Insertion**:
  - Medial aspect of the greater trochanter of femur.

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[hip_joint]].
- **Primary Actions**: [[hip_external_rotation]], horizontal [[hip_abduction]] (when hip is flexed), and dynamic acetabular stabilization.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[deep_front_line]] (DFL)**: Connects pelvic floor fascia (levator ani) to the deep external hip rotators.
