# Multifidus

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Multifidus** is a series of short, triangular fasciculi located in the groove on either side of the spinous processes along the entire length of the spine. Thickest in the lumbar region, it is the key segmental deep local stabilizer (Bergmark classification) responsible for intersegmental stiffness, proprioception, and controlling shear forces during multi-planar torso motion.

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin (Inferior/Proximal Attachments)**:
  - Sacral region: posterior surface of [[sacrum]], posterior superior iliac spine (PSIS), and dorsal sacroiliac ligaments.
  - Lumbar region: mammillary processes of all lumbar vertebrae (L1-L5).
  - Thoracic region: transverse processes of all thoracic vertebrae (T1-T12).
  - Cervical region: articular processes of C4-C7.
- **Insertion (Superior/Distal Attachments)**:
  - Lateral and lower margins of the spinous processes of vertebrae located 2 to 4 segments above the origin point.

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[lumbar_spine]], [[thoracic_spine]], [[cervical_spine]], [[sacrum]].
- **Stabilizer Function**:
  - Feedforward local stabilizer activating in anticipation of limb movement alongside [[transversus_abdominis]] and the [[diaphragm]].
  - Bilateral contraction: spinal extension and intersegmental compression stiffness.
  - Unilateral contraction: contralateral [[thoracic_rotation]] / axial rotation stabilization and lateral flexion control.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[deep_front_line]] (DFL)**: Forms the posterior deep vertebral boundary of the core cylinder.
- **[[thoracolumbar_fascia]] (TLF)**: Enclosed within the middle and anterior layers of the TLF, directly tensioning the fascial envelope during isometric spinal bracing.
