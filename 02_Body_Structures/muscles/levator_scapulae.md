# Levator Scapulae

## 📖 Definition & Overview

The **Levator Scapulae** is situated at the back and side of the neck. It links the upper [[cervical_spine]] (C1-C4) directly to the superior angle of the scapula, serving as a primary postural elevator and downward rotator of the scapula.

---

## 🦴 Bony Attachments & Anatomical Landmarks

- **Origin (Proximal Attachments)**:
  - Posterior tubercles of the transverse processes of the atlas (C1), axis (C2), C3, and C4 cervical vertebrae.
- **Insertion (Distal Attachments)**:
  - Superior angle and superior portion of the vertebral (medial) border of the scapula (above the spine of the scapula).

---

## ⚙️ Biomechanical Role & Joint Mechanics

- **Joints Acted Upon**: [[cervical_spine]], scapulothoracic articulation / [[shoulder_joint]].
- **Primary Actions**:
  - Scapular elevation and downward rotation.
  - Unilateral neck lateral flexion and ipsilateral cervical rotation.
  - Bilateral neck extension and cervical lordosis stabilization.

---

## 🕸️ Myofascial Integration

- **[[deep_back_arm_line]] (DBAL)**: Links cervical transverse processes to the medial scapula, rotator cuff, and triceps.
- **[[spiral_line]] Continuity**: Connects cervical tracks through the rhomboids to the contralateral serratus anterior.
