# Pivoting Moment

## Definition

The **pivoting moment** is the vertical-axis component of the external moment produced by the individual-foot [[ground_reaction_force|GRF]] vectors about the combined [[center_of_pressure|COP]]. A vector description is:

$$M_{pivot,z}=\sum_i\left[(\mathbf{r}_{combined\ COP\rightarrow COP_i}\times\mathbf{F}_{i,horizontal})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\right]$$

It is a moment from separated force vectors, not the direct torsional [[foot_contact_moment]] measured at a foot.

Do not add this pivoting component to a full foot-by-foot moment sum about golfer COM. The vertical-axis part of that sum already contains the separated-foot force couple, so doing both would double-count pivoting.

## Why It Matters

Kwon's Level 3 model identifies this as an important vertical-axis golfer-ground interaction moment. Its sign and magnitude depend on the foot-specific 3D forces and their geometry, not on the appearance of pelvis or foot rotation.

## Supporting Evidence

[[dr_kwon_golfer_ground_interaction]] anchors the definition to `kwon_fgmom`, “Pivoting Moments,” Figure 5, and reports the study boundary for the skilled-male sample.

## Measurement Requirements

Two synchronised force plates are required to resolve the 3D GRF and COP for each foot and calculate their moment about combined COP. A single plate under both feet or a pressure mat cannot provide the required foot-specific 3D vectors.

Ordinary single-camera video cannot measure this moment. Apparent foot direction, pelvis rotation, or body motion is not a substitute for force-plate kinetics.

## Phase Relevance

The moment can be integrated across source-defined swing intervals to form a contribution to [[angular_impulse]]. Phase comparisons require explicit event definitions and consistent axes; a visible rotational event alone is not a kinetic measurement.
