# Squat Myofascial Mapping

## Evidence Status

Anatomy Trains defines the structural continuities below but does not provide a
specific squat-analysis or squat-scoring model. Searching the source PDF found only
a general example of prolonged squatting as connective-tissue demand, not a squat
mechanics protocol. Every metric-to-line relationship in this node is therefore
`engine_synthesis` (Level 5), not direct evidence of line restriction or loading.

## V1 Mapping

| Observable pattern | Anatomical continuity | Candidate line hypothesis | View |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heel rise, ankle strategy, hip-depth relationship, posterior trunk support | plantar foot, calf, hamstrings, sacral region, spinal extensors | [[superficial_back_line]] | side |
| Knee-extension contribution and anterior lower-leg/trunk organization | anterior lower leg, quadriceps region, abdominal wall | [[superficial_front_line]] | side |
| Medial foot/knee organization, adductor-pelvis relationship, deep trunk support | inner foot/deep leg, adductors, psoas/diaphragm region | [[deep_front_line]] | front plus side |
| Pelvic shift and frontal-plane asymmetry | lateral foot/leg, IT region, lateral pelvis and trunk | [[lateral_line]] | front |
| Foot rotation, knee-to-foot tracking, pelvis/trunk rotation | foot, leg, pelvis, oblique trunk | [[spiral_line]] | front/back |

Cross-view synthesis may connect a side-view sagittal strategy with a front-view
frontal strategy only as a multi-plane `engine_synthesis`. For example, side-view
heel rise plus front-view knee-to-foot excursion may suggest an ankle-foot theme
involving SBL, DFL, LL, or Spiral Line continuities. It does not prove one line is
restricted, loaded, or causal.

[[functional_lines]] are not a primary V1 interpretation for a symmetrical unloaded
squat. Reserve them for loaded, asymmetric, rotational, or upper-limb-coupled squat
variants.

## Required Report Language

Use:

- "may be consistent with organization along";
- "suggests a possible loading strategy involving";
- "the anatomical continuity includes";
- "requires direct assessment to confirm".

Never use:

- line restriction detected;
- tight, blocked, weak, inactive, or overloaded line;
- measured fascial loading;
- the line caused the squat pattern.

## Competing-Explanation Rule

Every line hypothesis must be accompanied by joint-level and protocol alternatives.
For example, heel rise can reflect selected depth, stance, balance strategy, footwear,
camera error, or ankle-foot contribution; it is not an SBL diagnosis.
