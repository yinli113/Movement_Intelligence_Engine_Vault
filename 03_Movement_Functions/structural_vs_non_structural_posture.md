# Structural vs Non-Structural Posture

## Definition

The **structural vs non-structural** differential distinguishes functional (non-structural) sagittal misalignments — flexible, driven by habit and muscle hypo/hyperactivity, correcting on active extension — from structural ones (Scheuermann, idiopathic scoliosis, vertebral malformation, spondylolisthesis) that do not correct and require clinical referral. **Flexibility testing** distinguishes the two (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.2-3).

## Why it matters

This differential governs whether a posture finding is reportable as a correctable pattern (non-structural) or must be flagged for clinical referral (structural). A 2D app cannot perform the flexibility test, so it must keep this distinction as a hypothesis and a referral flag, not a verdict.

## Source-derived model

- Non-structural: flexible, corrects on active extension, functional aetiology.
- Structural: rigid, does not correct, anatomical aetiology (e.g., Scheuermann).
- Flexibility tests (e.g., forward-bend correction, active extension) separate them.

## Joint involvement

Spinal segments and pelvis primarily; the differential is clinical, not joint-specific.

## Muscle involvement

Non-structural involves muscle hypo/hyperactivity; structural involves anatomy. No specific muscle claims from this node.

## What a 2D app can observe

- The sagittal silhouette that *suggests* a posture type; it cannot observe flexibility/correctability.

## What the app must not infer

- That a posture is structural or non-structural from a silhouette alone.
- Diagnosis or the need for/absence of clinical referral without flexibility testing.
