# Moment Arm

## Definition

The **Moment Arm** ($d$ or $r_{\perp}$) is the perpendicular distance between the axis about which an object rotates (in human movement, usually a joint center or the body's Center of Mass) and the line of action of the force applied to create rotation.

Mathematically, if the force vector $F$ is applied at a position vector $r$ relative to the axis:

$$d = r \sin(\theta)$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the force vector and the position vector. The torque generated is:

$$\tau = F \times d$$

---

## Why It Matters

A longer moment arm multiplies the rotational torque generated by a given force. In the golf swing, the body relies on structural moment arms to generate high rotational velocities without requiring excessive muscular effort. Stance width, pelvic dimensions, and the spatial relationship between the **Center of Mass (COM)** and the **Center of Pressure (COP)** define the primary moment arms of the swing.

---

## Biomechanical Relevance (Level 3 Evidence)

In golf, there are two primary moment arms that determine rotational performance:

### 1. COM-COP Separation Moment Arm
- **Vertical Force Lever**: When a golfer pushes down vertically into the lead foot during transition, that force vector acts through the lead foot's COP. The horizontal distance between the body's COM and the lead COP is the moment arm.
- **Effect**: By shifting the COM laterally target-ward during the downswing while keeping the lead COP wide, the golfer increases this horizontal moment arm. Pushing down vertically now creates a massive rotation-generating moment, accelerating pelvic turn.

### 2. Muscle Moment Arms
- The physical distance between a tendon's line of action and the center of the joint it crosses (e.g., the distance from the patellar tendon to the knee axis).
- A larger muscle moment arm allows a muscle to generate greater joint torque for the same contractile force, although it reduces the joint's maximum angular range of motion per unit of muscle shortening.

## App Hypotheses & App Logic (Level 5 Evidence)

### Metrics Integration
The horizontal distance between the COM and COP is quantified by the **COM–COP Separation Score (CCS)** in the app.

### MediaPipe Proxies
Since cameras cannot see force lines or exact pressure centers, the app estimates the COM-COP moment arm using:
- **Pelvic Center to Lead Foot Distance**: Pelvic center acts as a proxy for COM, and the lead ankle joint acts as a proxy for the COP under the lead foot. The lateral separation between the pelvic center and the lead ankle during transition provides a geometric estimation of the moment arm.
