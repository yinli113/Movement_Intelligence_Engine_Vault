# Flat-Back Posture

## Definition

**Flat-back posture** is a non-structural sagittal posture type defined by **reduced lumbar lordosis (flattened lower back) and posterior pelvic tilt**, with body-part positions specified per the source's table (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.5-11). It is read as a pattern hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

## Why it matters

It is the "low curve" counterpart to the lordotic type and is easily missed because the silhouette looks "neutral." It illustrates that reduced curvature is itself a posture type requiring a functional-organisation reading, not an absence of pattern.

## Source-derived model

- Body-part positions: reduced lumbar lordosis, posterior pelvic tilt, hip extension tendency, often slight forward trunk lean.
- Functional organisation (per source table): lumbar extensors lengthened-appearing/hypoactive; hip flexors lengthened-appearing; hamstrings may be shortened-appearing/hyperactive.
- Corrective logic: regain lumbar extensor activity and restore curve, not flatten further.

## Joint involvement

[[lumbar_spine]] (reduced lordosis), pelvis (posterior tilt), [[hip_joint]], [[knee_joint]].

## Muscle involvement

Per the source's flat-back table (p.5-11): lumbar extensors, hip flexors (lengthened-appearing/hypoactive); hamstrings (shortened-appearing/hyperactive). Functional-organisation hypotheses, not measured states.
