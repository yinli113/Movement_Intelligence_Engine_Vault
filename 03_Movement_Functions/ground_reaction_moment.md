# Ground Reaction Moment

## Definition

The **Ground Reaction Moment (GRM)** (also referred to by Dr. Kwon as **FGMOM** or Golfer-Ground Interaction Moment) is the rotational torque generated about the body's Center of Mass (COM) by external ground reaction forces. 

Mathematically, the moment ($M$) generated by a force vector ($F_{\text{GRF}}$) acting at the Center of Pressure (COP) about the COM is defined as:

$$M = r \times F_{\text{GRF}}$$

where:
- $r$ is the position vector from the COM to the COP (the moment arm).
- $F_{\text{GRF}}$ is the Ground Reaction Force vector.
- $\times$ denotes the vector cross product.

---

## Why It Matters

In golf, rotation is the primary driver of clubhead velocity. The body cannot rotate itself in a vacuum; it requires an external torque to change its **angular momentum**. The Ground Reaction Moment is that external torque. Pushing into the ground generates reaction forces that, when acting at a distance from the body's Center of Mass, create the rotational moments that turn the hips, torso, and ultimately swing the club.

## Biomechanical Breakdown (Level 3 Evidence)

According to Dr. Kwon's analysis, golfer-ground interaction moments are divided into three components based on the planes of action:

### 1. Frontal Plane Moment
- **Role**: Drives pelvic rotation and lateral tilt in the downswing.
- **Significance**: Dr. Kwon identifies this as the largest contributor to downswing rotation. It is generated by pushing down hard with the lead leg while pushing up/unweighting the trail side, creating a couple (rotational force pair).

### 2. Sagittal Plane Moment
- **Role**: Prevents the golfer's body from collapsing forward during the high-velocity downswing and impact.
- **Significance**: Reaches its maximum at or near ball impact, stabilizing the spine and pelvis against the forward pull of the club.

### 3. Horizontal Plane Moment
- **Role**: Direct twisting/pivoting against the ground.
- **Significance**: Surprisingly small in magnitude compared to the frontal and sagittal moments; it plays a secondary role in generating total body rotation.

### 4. The Vertical Force Contribution
A common misconception in golf coaching is that vertical forces (pushing straight down/jumping up) only move the body vertically and do not help rotate it. Dr. Kwon disproves this:
> *"One thing clearly shown in Figure 2 is that even a vertical GRF [Ground Reaction Force] can generate a moment about the COM [Center of Mass] as long as the force vector does not pass through the COM. In other words, **pushing the ground down hard can affect the rotational motion of the body/club and thus contributes to the clubhead speed.**"*
> (Source: [drkwongolf.info/biom/fgmom.html (Dr. Kwon Golf Biomechanics)](http://drkwongolf.info/biom/fgmom.html#:~:text=One%20thing%20clearly%20shown%20in,contributes%20to%20the%20clubhead%20speed.))

As long as the lead foot pushes vertically down at a point that is offset horizontally from the Center of Mass, it creates a large rotational moment arm, driving pelvic rotation.

## App Hypotheses & App Logic (Level 5 Evidence)

### Metrics Integration
The efficacy of GRM generation is evaluated in the app via:
1. **Torque Generation Score (TGS)**: Measures how effectively the golfer converts vertical and horizontal GRF into torque around the COM.
2. **COM-COP Separation Score (CCS)**: Evaluates the horizontal distance (moment arm) between the COM and COP during the transition, which multiplies the rotational moment.

### MediaPipe Proxies
Because standard cameras cannot measure forces directly, the app infers GRM using:
- **Pelvic Lateral Sway vs. Foot Stance Width**: Used to estimate the moment arm ($r$).
- **Vertical Pelvic Displacement Rate**: Rapid pelvic drop followed by rapid extension (jumping) acts as a proxy for vertical force magnitude ($F_{\text{GRF}}$).
