# Kyphotic Posture

## Definition

**Kyphotic posture** is a non-structural sagittal posture type defined by **increased thoracic kyphosis and forward head position**, with body-part positions specified per the source's table (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.5-11). It is read as a pattern hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

## Why it matters

It is a common posture type for desk-bound populations and a clean 2D-observable silhouette. It illustrates the [[apparent_shortness_vs_structural_shortening]] rule: apparent pectoral/chest shortening may reflect hyperactivity, not contracture.

## Source-derived model

- Body-part positions: increased thoracic kyphosis, forward head, protracted shoulders.
- Functional organisation (per source table): thoracic extensors and deep cervical flexors lengthened-appearing/hypoactive; pectorals and cervical extensors shortened-appearing/hyperactive.
- Corrective logic: regain thoracic extensor / deep cervical flexor activity, reduce pectoral/cervical-extensor overactivity.

## Joint involvement

[[thoracic_spine]] (hyperkyphosis), [[cervical_spine]] (forward head), [[shoulder_joint]] (protraction), [[lumbar_spine]].

## Muscle involvement

Per the source's kyphotic table (p.5-11): thoracic extensors, deep cervical flexors (hypoactive/lengthened-appearing); pectorals, cervical extensors (hyperactive/shortened-appearing). Functional-organisation hypotheses, not measured states.

## Movement or phase relationships

## Possible myofascial relationships

Cross-linking the kyphotic silhouette to a fascial line (e.g., a shortened-appearing Superficial Front Line at the chest) is an **engine synthesis (C)** from Anatomy Trains. Label it as a hypothesis.

## What a 2D app can observe

- Sagittal silhouette: thoracic curve depth, head position, shoulder protraction — sufficient to propose the kyphotic type as a hypothesis.

## What the app must not infer

- That specific muscles are shortened/hypoactive from a silhouette alone.
- Diagnosis, pain source, or treatment without clinical assessment.
- Structural vs non-structural without flexibility testing.
