# Sway-Back Posture

## Definition

**Sway-back posture** is a non-structural sagittal posture type defined by **posterior displacement of the trunk relative to the pelvis with the hips in extension**, with body-part positions specified per the source's table (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.5-11). It is read as a pattern hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

## Why it matters

It is easily confused with the flat-back or lordotic types because the silhouette is subtle; the defining feature is the trunk-pelvis displacement and hip extension. Correct identification matters because its functional-organisation hypothesis differs from the other types.

## Source-derived model

- Body-part positions: trunk posterior to pelvis, hips extended, pelvis anteriorly tilted relative to the trunk, often reduced lumbar lordosis.
- Functional organisation (per source table): hip flexors lengthened-appearing/hypoactive; abdominals and hamstrings may be shortened-appearing/hyperactive.
- Corrective logic: regain hip-flexor and abdominal stabiliser activity, reduce hamstring overactivity.

## Joint involvement

[[hip_joint]] (extension), pelvis, [[lumbar_spine]], [[knee_joint]] (often hyperextension).

## Muscle involvement

Per the source's sway-back table (p.5-11): hip flexors (lengthened-appearing/hypoactive); abdominals, hamstrings (shortened-appearing/hyperactive). Functional-organisation hypotheses, not measured states.

- Sagittal silhouette: trunk-pelvis displacement, hip extension, knee hyperextension, pelvic position — sufficient to propose the sway-back type as a hypothesis.

- Diagnosis, pain source, or treatment without clinical assessment.
- Structural vs non-structural without flexibility testing.
