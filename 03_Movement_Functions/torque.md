# Torque

## Definition

**Torque** ($\tau$) is the rotational equivalent of linear force. It is a vector quantity that measures the tendency of a force to rotate an object about an axis, fold, or pivot. 

Mathematically, torque is defined as:

$$\tau = r \times F = r F \sin(\theta)$$

where:
- $r$ is the position vector from the axis of rotation to the point where the force is applied (the moment arm).
- $F$ is the force vector.
- $\theta$ is the angle between the position vector and the force vector.

In human biomechanics, torque is split into:
1. **External Torque**: Forces acting on the body from the outside (e.g., Ground Reaction Moments, club weight).
2. **Internal Torque**: Forces generated inside the body (e.g., muscle contractions pulling on bone segments via tendons).

---

## Why It Matters

In rotational sports like golf, the body must generate torque to turn segments at high speed. A golfer cannot accelerate their pelvis, thorax, or club without generating torque. The swing transition is a process of converting ground-generated external torque (GRMs) into skeletal segment rotation, which is then amplified by internal muscular torque to whip the clubhead through impact.

## Biomechanical Breakdown (Level 3 Evidence)

### 1. Pelvic Torque
- In the downswing, pelvic torque is generated by the force couple of the lower limbs: one hip pushing forward/up and the other pulling back/down.
- This creates the initial axial rotation torque that starts the kinematic sequence.

### 2. Torso Torque (Core Sling Coupling)
- The oblique abdominal muscles and the [[functional_lines]] transmit rotational torque between the pelvis and the thoracic spine.
- Tension in the thoracolumbar fascia acts as a spring, transferring pelvic torque into the upper torso.

## App Hypotheses & App Logic (Level 5 Evidence)

### Metrics Integration
Torque efficiency and conversion are measured by the **Torque Generation Score (TGS)** in the app.

### MediaPipe Proxies
Because internal muscle torques cannot be measured, the app uses kinematics:
- **Segment Angular Acceleration**: The rate of change of pelvic and thoracic angular velocities. High angular acceleration implies high net torque.
