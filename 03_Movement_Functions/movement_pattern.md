# Movement Pattern

## Definition

A **movement pattern** is a whole-body, multi-joint coordinated action — squat, step, lunge, reach, rotate, push-up — treated as the primary unit of analysis, ahead of isolated joint motion (Gray Cook, *Movement*, Ch. 1-2). It is the concrete expression of the [[movement_vs_motion]] principle.

## Why it matters

Patterns are domain-neutral: a squat, lunge, or single-leg step appears in golf, gait, posture, and squat assessment alike. Reasoning at the pattern level keeps the engine cross-domain and prevents single-joint over-interpretation.

## Source-derived model

- Fundamental patterns: deep squat, hurdle step, inline lunge, shoulder mobility/reach, active straight-leg raise, trunk stability push-up, rotary stability.
- Patterns are screened (FMS) before being assessed (SFMA).

## Joint involvement

Each pattern loads a specific multi-joint chain; see the child pattern nodes.

## Muscle involvement

Patterns recruit coordinated muscle systems; muscle-level claims are out of scope for this node.

## Movement or phase relationships

Patterns compose the [[functional_movement_screen]] (A) and sit under the sport-skill layer of the [[performance_pyramid]] (A).

## Possible myofascial relationships

None directly. Mapping a pattern to a fascial line is an engine synthesis (C).

## What a 2D app can observe

- The pattern's joint-angle and alignment proxies from a 2D view.

## What the app must not infer

- Underlying mobility/stability deficit, muscle activation, fascial tension, or diagnosis from the pattern alone.
