# Psoas-Diaphragm Core Complex (Myers' Cobra)

## Definition

The **Psoas-Diaphragm Core Complex ("The Cobra")** is an [[anatomy_trains_myofascial_thomas_w_myers]] anatomical and clinical model describing the continuous functional myofascial core of the [[deep_front_line]]. Established formally in [[myers_cobra_core_psoas_diaphragm_2019]], it details how the [[psoas_major]] muscle and the respiratory [[diaphragm]] connect intimately in front of the T12–L2 spinal junction via the diaphragmatic crura and the medial/lateral arcuate ligaments.

In this model:
- The **body and tail** of each cobra is the psoas major on either side, wrapping from the lesser trochanter of the femur over the pelvic brim to attach broadly along the T12–L5 vertebral bodies, discs, and transverse processes.
- The **hood and head** of each cobra is the corresponding dome of the respiratory diaphragm, tying down into the spine at the crura.
- The **tip of the cobra's nose** rests where the rectus abdominis borders the anterior lower costal margin (ribs 6–7).

## Sagittal Core Presentations

In static posture analysis and clinical bodyreading, Myers distinguishes two primary sagittal departures from vertical axial poise:

### 1. The Depressed Cobra (Sway-Back / Core Collapse)
In this presentation (corresponding to Myers 2019 Image 4C and closely related to [[sway_back_posture]]):
- **Deep Core Collapse:** The psoas major loses upward resting tone and becomes functionally inhibited / depressed.
- **Thoracolumbar Fall-Back:** Without upward axial lift from the DFL, the upper lumbar spine (T12–L2) falls posteriorly into a flat or kyphotic hinge.
- **Anterior Dome Slump:** The diaphragm dome slumps down and forward ("the nose of the cobra falls in front"), depressing the anterior lower ribcage and pushing the visceral contents outward.
- **Posterior Guy-Wire Hypertonicity:** The posterior tissues—especially [[quadratus_lumborum]] and the thoracolumbar fascia—thicken into hypertonic emergency guy-wires to arrest further backward trunk displacement.
- **Forward Head Compensation:** To prevent falling backward and keep the eyes horizontal, the cervical spine extends and the head translates forward (forward head posture; see [[neck_tension]]). Treating the neck in isolation inevitably fails because cervical tension is secondary to the thoracolumbar fall-back below.
- **Pelvic Relationship:** Often co-occurs with anterior pelvic translation relative to the feet and posterior pelvic tilt bias, holding the psoas **locked-long** rather than locked-short.

### 2. The Over-Enthusiastic Cobra (Anterior Lordotic Pull)
In this presentation (Myers 2019 Image 4D):
- The psoas major is hypertonic and over-recruited.
- The upper lumbar spine is pulled anteriorly into excessive lordosis.
- The diaphragm dome is tipped backward, lifting the anterior costal margin and exposing the "throat" of the cobra.

## Clinical Handling Precaution (Myers' Rule)

> [!CAUTION]
> In a **Depressed Cobra** presentation, the psoas major is **lengthened, inhibited, and exhausted**, NOT locked-short.
> - **Contraindication:** Aggressive deep stripping, digging, or painful ischemic compression into the psoas is contraindicated and will further destabilize the core.
> - **Protocol:** 
>   1. Release and free the hypertonic posterior guy-wires ([[quadratus_lumborum]] and thoracolumbar fascia).
>   2. Mobilize the diaphragmatic dome and restore 360° ribcage breathing excursion.
>   3. Revivify and tone the psoas with gentle active neuromuscular recruitment (e.g. water wading, supine hip flexion >90° with minimal TFL substitution, banded hip flexion with extended knee).

## App Observational Translation

A 2D computer vision posture app:
1. Cannot directly see the psoas, diaphragm crura, or autonomic nerves.
2. Observes the sagittal silhouette: active-side shoulder-to-pelvis plumbline inclination (backward vs. forward trunk lean) coupled with cranio-cervical tilt (neck angle) and sagittal head translation.
3. When backward trunk lean co-occurs with forward head translation and posterior pelvic tilt bias, the app hypothesizes a **Depressed Cobra / Sway-Back kinetic chain** (Level-5 hypothesis).
4. The app frames this as a clinical reading question with palpation and movement confirmation checks, never as a medical diagnosis.
