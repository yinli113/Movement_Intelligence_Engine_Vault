# Mobility-Stability Relationship

## Definition

**Mobility** is the available range at a joint or tissue (extensibility). **Stability** is motor control within that range. The two are distinct, and corrective logic establishes **mobility before stability** (Gray Cook, *Movement*, Ch. 8, Appendix 1). This is the operational core of the [[joint_by_joint_concept]].

## Why it matters

A visible range limitation (mobility proxy) and a motor-control failure (stability) look similar on camera but require different actions. Separating them is essential to honest reporting and to the [[selective_functional_movement_assessment]] breakout logic.

## Source-derived model

- Mobility = passive range / extensibility.
- Stability = active motor control within range.
- Corrective order: mobility first, then stability.

## Joint involvement

Whole chain; each joint's primary need (mobility or stability) is set by the [[joint_by_joint_concept]].

## Muscle involvement

Stability implicates local stabilisers; mobility implicates extensibility. No specific muscle claims from this node.

## Movement or phase relationships

Underlies the SFMA mobility-vs-stability breakout (A) and the [[joint_by_joint_concept]] (A).

## Possible myofascial relationships

None directly. Linking a mobility deficit to a fascial line is an engine synthesis (C).

## What a 2D app can observe

- A mobility proxy (visible end-range) and a stability proxy (holding/alignment during a pattern).

## What the app must not infer

- Whether a limitation is mobility or stability (requires hands-on assessment).
- Muscle length, activation, or fascial tension.
