# Regional Interdependence

## Definition

**Regional interdependence** is the principle that a movement problem at one region may be driven by a deficit or compensation at a distant region, so assessment must consider the whole chain (Gray Cook, *Movement*, Appendix 1; underlies the [[joint_by_joint_concept]] and SFMA breakouts).

## Why it matters

This is why the engine reads patterns, not isolated joints: a knee problem may reflect an ankle or hip limitation. It also bounds what a 2D app can claim — it can *suggest* a distant driver as a hypothesis, not confirm it.

## Source-derived model

- Distant-region drivers can cause or maintain a local problem.
- Whole-chain assessment precedes local treatment.

## Joint involvement

Whole kinetic chain; the [[joint_by_joint_concept]] maps where mobility/stability needs alternate.

## Muscle involvement

No specific muscle claims from this node.

## Movement or phase relationships

Underlies the [[selective_functional_movement_assessment]] breakouts (A) and the [[joint_by_joint_concept]] (A).

## Possible myofascial relationships

None directly. Linking a distant driver to a fascial line is an engine synthesis (C).

## What a 2D app can observe

- Patterns across multiple joints simultaneously, enabling a distant-region *hypothesis*.

## What the app must not infer

- That a distant region is the confirmed cause of a local problem.
- Muscle, fascial, or kinetic causation from 2D alone.
