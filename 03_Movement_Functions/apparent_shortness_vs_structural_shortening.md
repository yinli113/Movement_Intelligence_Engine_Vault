# Apparent Shortness vs Structural Shortening

## Definition

The **apparent shortness vs structural shortening** rule states that visible posture or reduced flexibility does **not** prove true muscle-fibre shortening: **lengthening ≠ weakness** and **shortening ≠ contracture**. Flexibility tests (popliteal angle, long-sitting) and posture type can disagree; the resolution is the functional-organisation reading, not a length-only rule (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.12-13).

## Why it matters

This is the single most important cautionary rule for a static-posture app: it forbids the common error of reading a silhouette as "this muscle is shortened, stretch it." The hamstrings example shows a lengthened hamstring can be weak (hypoactive) and a shortened-appearing muscle can be hyperactive, not contractured.

## Source-derived model

- Lengthened ≠ weak; shortened ≠ contractured.
- Flexibility test vs posture type can disagree.
- Resolution: functional-organisation reading (stabiliser hypoactivity / mobiliser hyperactivity), not length-only.
- Corrective exercise works in internal (not full) range of motion to regain stabiliser activity.

## Joint involvement

Applies across joints; it is a reporting rule, not a joint model.

## Muscle involvement

The rule is *about* muscle state but forbids asserting it from a silhouette. Specific muscle claims are traced to per-type tables in the child posture nodes.

## What a 2D app can observe

- The silhouette that *suggests* apparent shortness/length; it cannot measure muscle-fibre length or contracture.

## What the app must not infer

- That a muscle is structurally shortened or lengthened from a silhouette.
- Fascial tension, muscle length, activation, or diagnosis.
