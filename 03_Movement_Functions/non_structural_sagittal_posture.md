# Non-Structural Sagittal Posture

## Definition

**Non-structural sagittal posture** is the Czaprowski taxonomy of sagittal-plane posture misalignments — [[lordotic_posture]], [[kyphotic_posture]], [[kyphotic_lordotic_posture]], [[flat_back_posture]], [[sway_back_posture]] — that are **functional** (habit + muscle hypo/hyperactivity) and correctable, as distinct from structural misalignments (Czaprowski et al., 2018, p.1-3). It is the engine's canonical static-posture taxonomy and the source of the app's guiding question.

## Why it matters

This taxonomy reframes posture from a static label ("what posture do you have?") to a movement-organisation question ("how does your body organise movement?"). It pairs with Anatomy Trains [[bodyreading_static_posture]] (fascial-line reading) to form a two-layer posture interpretation.

## Source-derived model

- Four (+1 combined) sagittal types, each defined by body-part positions and by lengthened/shortened + hypoactive/hyperactive muscle tables.
- Non-structural = correctable via functional reorganisation; structural = requires clinical differentiation (e.g., Scheuermann).
- Corrective logic: regain stabiliser activity, reduce mobiliser overactivity, in internal range of motion.

## Joint involvement

Head, cervical/thoracic/lumbar spine, pelvis, hip, knee, ankle — each type specifies positions per body part.

## Muscle involvement

Per-type muscle tables (lengthened/shortened, hypoactive/hyperactive); functional classification via [[stabilizer_mobilizer_classification]] (A). Specific muscle claims belong to the child posture-type nodes, traced to the source's tables.

## Movement or phase relationships

Parent of the five posture types (A) and the [[structural_vs_non_structural_posture]] distinction (A); complements [[bodyreading_static_posture]] (B).

## Possible myofascial relationships

Cross-linking a posture type to a fascial line is an engine synthesis (C): e.g., a lordotic silhouette may *suggest* shortening in a fascial line, but fascial-line reading comes from Anatomy Trains, not this source.

## What a 2D app can observe

- Sagittal silhouette: head, spinal curves, pelvic tilt, hip/knee/ankle positions — sufficient to classify the type as a hypothesis.

## What the app must not infer

- That the visible posture proves muscle-fibre shortening (see [[apparent_shortness_vs_structural_shortening]]).
- Diagnosis, pain source, or treatment without clinical assessment.
- Structural vs non-structural without flexibility testing.
