# Active Straight-Leg Raise (FMS Test 5)

## Definition

The **active straight-leg raise** lifts one leg straight while the other stays flat, screening active [[hip_joint]] flexion mobility of the raising leg and control of the opposite limb, scored 0-3 per side (Gray Cook, *Movement*, Ch. 6, Ch. 9). FMS test 5.

## Why it matters

It isolates hip-flexion mobility in an active (not passive) context, relevant to gait swing and any sport hip demand. It is a clean 2D-observable pattern.

## Source-derived model

- Active raise of one straight leg, opposite leg flat; measure the raise angle against thresholds.
- Scored 0-3 per side; asymmetry reduces the composite score.

## Joint involvement

[[hip_joint]] flexion of the raising leg; pelvic control of the base.

## Muscle involvement

No muscle claims from this node. Inferring hamstring length or neural tension from the raise is out of scope (C).

## Movement or phase relationships

A fundamental [[movement_pattern]] (A); hip-flexion range relates to gait swing limb clearance (C).

## Possible myofascial relationships

None directly. Fascial-line mapping is an engine synthesis (C).

## What a 2D app can observe

- The raise angle (hip-flexion proxy) per side from a 2D side view.

## What the app must not infer

- Hamstring length, neural tension, or psoas activation.
- Diagnosis or treatment.
