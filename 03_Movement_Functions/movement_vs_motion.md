# Movement versus Motion

## Definition

**Movement** is a whole-body, multi-system pattern (mobility, stability, motor control, perception). **Motion** is the isolated change of a single joint or segment. The engine reasons about movement, not motion alone (Gray Cook, *Movement*, Ch. 1).

## Why it matters

This distinction is the engine's first interpretive filter. A camera can measure motion (a knee angle), but a movement claim requires the pattern context (what the ankle, hip, and trunk do at the same time). It prevents the common error of treating a single-joint descriptor as a movement conclusion.

## Source-derived model

- Movement = whole-body pattern across multiple systems.
- Motion = single-segment change.
- Optimal movement = coordinated interplay, not summed isolated motions.

## Joint involvement

Whole chain; no single joint defines movement.

## Muscle involvement

Movement recruits coordinated muscle systems; isolated motion recruits a single segment. No muscle-level claims from this node.
