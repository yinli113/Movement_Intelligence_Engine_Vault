# Squat Switch Failure Modes

## Overview

In Thomas Myers' *Anatomy Trains*, a **Switch** is a dynamic fascial junction where tension can take more than one path depending on joint position, postural alignment, or muscular engagement. In a complex closed-kinetic-chain movement like a bodyweight squat, several key switches derail or fail to transition tension smoothly, forcing the kinetic chain into camera-observable kinematic misalignments.

This node defines the 5 primary dynamic switches involved in squat mechanics. All switch failure relationships are **Level 5 Engine Synthesis** hypotheses and do not constitute direct measurement of tissue tension or pathological diagnosis.

---

## 1. The Knee Switch (SBL Gastrocnemius-Hamstring Derailment)

* **Associated Meridian:** [[superficial_back_line]] (SBL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Posterior knee joint where the two heads of [[gastrocnemius]] overlap with the distal tendons of [[biceps_femoris_long_head]], [[semitendinosus]], and [[semimembranosus]].
* **Normal Function:**
  * In extended knee stance ($0^\circ$), the SBL is locked into one continuous posterior cable.
  * In squat descent, knee flexion ($>30^\circ$) **derails** the knee switch, slackening the Gastrocnemius and allowing the hamstrings and calves to function independently.
* **Failure Mode (Derailment Failure):** If Soleus or Achilles tissue lacks compliance, or if Gastrocnemius fails to derail, the calf pulls down on the distal femur during knee flexion. This locks talocrural dorsiflexion prematurely, forcing **Early Heel Lift** or pulling the pelvis into backward tilt.

## 2. The Femoral Adductor Switch (DFL Anterior vs. Posterior Track)

* **Associated Meridian:** [[deep_front_line]] (DFL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Linea aspera and ischial ramus insertion of [[adductor_magnus]], [[adductor_longus]], and [[adductor_brevis]].
* **Normal Function:**
  * In standing posture ($0^\circ$ hip flexion), Adductor Longus and Brevis act as anterior hip flexors and medial rotators.
  * In deep squat descent ($>90^\circ$ hip flexion), the mechanical line of pull **switches**: Adductor Magnus transforms into the primary hip extensor, working in synergy with Gluteus Maximus.
* **Failure Mode (Adductor Over-Tension Switch):** If the anterior track (Adductor Longus/Brevis) fails to release while Adductor Magnus is hyper-tonic, the femur is pulled medially and internally rotated at peak depth, resulting in **Dynamic Knee Valgus** and **Butt Wink** (lumbar flexion).

---

## 3. The ASIS Anchor Switch (SFL Pelvic Station)

* **Associated Meridian:** [[superficial_front_line]] (SFL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS) station, linking [[rectus_femoris]] below to [[rectus_abdominis]] above.
* **Normal Function:** During squat descent, Rectus Femoris lengthens across the flexing knee. Rectus Abdominis must provide upward counter-tension on the ASIS to anchor the pelvic station.
* **Failure Mode (Anterior Pelvic Shift):** If Rectus Abdominis lacks isometric tone, the downward pull of Rectus Femoris tilts the pelvis anteriorly (**Anterior Pelvic Tilt** / "Duck Butt"), causing early lumbar hyper-lordosis and loss of intra-abdominal pressure.

---

## 4. The Medial Arch Stirrup Switch (DFL-SPL Foot Loop)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[deep_front_line]] (DFL) & [[spiral_line]] (SPL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Plantar foot stirrup formed by [[tibialis_anterior]] (SFL/SPL) and [[peroneus_longus]] (LL/SPL), supported internally by [[tibialis_posterior]] (DFL).
* **Normal Function:** Controls dynamic weight distribution across the 3-point foot tripod during depth changes.
* **Failure Mode (Eversion Shift):** If Tibialis Posterior (DFL) loses arch height control under load, tension switches into Peroneus Longus (SPL), pulling the 1st metatarsal into eversion and collapsing the medial longitudinal arch (**Excessive Foot Pronation**).

---

## 5. The Calcaneal Station Elevation Switch (SBL-DFL Ankle Block)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[superficial_back_line]] (SBL) & [[deep_front_line]] (DFL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Calcaneus bony station where Plantar Fascia connects into the Achilles Tendon and Soleus.
* **Normal Function:** Calcaneus remains grounded flat while tibia translates forward into ankle dorsiflexion.
* **Failure Mode (Elevation Bypass):** When Soleus (SBL Local) or Tibialis Posterior (DFL Local) hits terminal length before desired squat depth is achieved, the nervous system lifts the Calcaneal station off the ground (**Early Heel Lift**) to bypass the blocked ankle joint.

## Vault Report Boundary & Clinical Rules

When referencing switch failure modes in app reports:
1. **Never claim measured tissue state:** Use *"Findings may be consistent with a Femoral Adductor Switch failure"*, never *"Measured switch failure"*.
2. **Include competing joint explanations:** Always pair a switch failure hypothesis with joint-level alternatives (e.g. ankle joint capsular restriction vs. SBL calf tightness).
3. **Provide an actionable retest:** Every switch failure hypothesis must prompt an in-person movement retest (e.g. heel elevation wedge, mini-band resistance, stance width modification).
