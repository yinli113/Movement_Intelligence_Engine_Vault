# Ipsilateral Functional Line

## Definition

The **Ipsilateral Functional Line (IFL)** is a postural and movement sling running down the same side of the trunk. It connects the humerus (via latissimus dorsi) down across the lateral ribs to the external oblique, and then attaches to the pelvis at the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS), continuing down the front of the thigh via the sartorius.

---

## Why It Matters

In golf, the IFL is primarily active in **lateral stabilization, side-bending, and swing plane stabilization**. It resists excessive target-ward sliding (slide) by stabilizing the lateral column of the trunk, and controls spinal side-bend (right side-bend for right-handed golfers) during impact.

---

## Stable Anatomy (Level 1 & 2 Evidence)

The line tracks:
1. **Shaft of Humerus** (Latissimus Dorsi insertion)
2. **[[latissimus_dorsi]]** muscle
3. **[[external_oblique]]** muscle (ipsilateral)
4. **Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS)** of pelvis
5. **[[sartorius]]** muscle
6. **Pes Anserinus** (tibial attachment)

---

## Golf Biomechanics & Rotational Interpretation (Level 3 & 4 Evidence)

- **Side-Bend Stabilization**: During the downswing, the trail-side IFL actively controls and coordinates the lateral tilt (side-bend) of the spine. Pushing into the lead foot generates a vertical force couple that requires lateral trunk deceleration, which is supported by the lead-side IFL.
- **Sway Control**: By locking the ipsilateral hip to the torso, the IFL prevents the hips from swaying laterally during the backswing (trail side) and downswing (lead side).

---

## App Hypotheses & App Logic (Level 5 Evidence)

- **FLLI Integration**: IFL activity is evaluated by measuring the distance between the acromion and the ASIS on the same side of the body.
- **Sway detection**: High values of lateral pelvis-shoulder displacement (sway) suggest a failure of the IFL to stabilize the lateral column.
