# Golf Swing Switch Failure Modes

## Overview

In golf swing biomechanics (Dr. Young-Hoo Kwon & Bourgain et al. 2022 framework), energy transfers sequentially across kinetic segments (Feet/Ground $\rightarrow$ Pelvis $\rightarrow$ Thorax $\rightarrow$ Arms $\rightarrow$ Club). A **Golf Swing Switch** represents a critical dynamic junction where fascial tension transitions from loading to unloading, or from one diagonal sling to another across swing phases.

A classic swing flaw (Early Extension, Sway, Over-the-Top, Loss of Posture, Hanging Back, Casting) reflects a specific **Golf Swing Switch Failure Mode** where a line fails to anchor or transfer rotational torque.

All golf switch failure modes are **Level 5 Engine Synthesis** hypotheses. They describe camera-observable image-plane swing descriptors and do not constitute measured ground reaction forces (GRF), ground reaction moments (GRM at COP), 3D joint torques, or medical diagnoses.

---

## 1. Backswing Trail-Hip Loading Switch (SPL / LL Trail Anchor)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[spiral_line]] (SPL) & [[lateral_line]] (LL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Trail foot stirrup, Trail Greater Trochanter station, and Trail Pelvis.
* **Normal Function:** Trail Peroneals, Gluteus Medius, and Biceps Femoris anchor trail hip internal rotation and absorb backswing pelvic loading without lateral slide (`address_to_shaft_parallel` to `top_backswing`).
* **Failure Mode (Trail Sway Switch Failure):** If trail LL/SPL fails to anchor the trail hip, pelvis slides laterally away from the target (**Sway**), breaking the coil and forcing a reverse spine angle.

---

## 2. Transition Pelvis-Thorax X-Factor Dissociation Switch (BFL / FFL Stretch-Shortening)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[back_functional_line]] (BFL) & [[front_functional_line]] (FFL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Thoracolumbar Fascia, Pubic Symphysis, Contralateral Obliques, and Latissimus Dorsi.
* **Normal Function:** Pelvis initiates downswing transition before thorax finishes backswing, stretching the cross-torso BFL/FFL slings into viscoelastic pre-stress (**[[x_factor]] Stretch-Shortening Cycle**).
* **Failure Mode (Over-the-Top / Early Fire Switch):** If upper chest/shoulders fire prematurely before pelvic transition, the cross-torso switch fails, dumping upper body torque outward (**Over-the-Top** / Steep Downswing).

---

## 3. Impact Lead-Leg Post & Ground Transfer Switch (DFL / LL Lead Post)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[deep_front_line]] (DFL) & [[lateral_line]] (LL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Lead Navicular/Arch, Lead Tibialis Posterior, Lead Adductor Magnus, Lead Gluteus Medius.
* **Normal Function:** Lead leg posts firmly into hip internal rotation, extension, and abduction at impact (`max_unweighting_to_impact`), acting as a braking wall to transfer linear impulse into angular momentum.
* **Failure Mode (Early Extension / Hanging Back Switch):** 
  * *Early Extension:* Lead DFL adductor/psoas fails to centrate hip $\rightarrow$ pelvis thrusts forward toward ball.
  * *Hanging Back:* Weight fails to transfer to lead foot DFL arch $\rightarrow$ golfer remains on trail foot at impact.

---

## 4. Follow-Through Deceleration Switch (SBL / SPL Deceleration Sling)

* **Associated Meridians:** [[superficial_back_line]] (SBL) & [[spiral_line]] (SPL)
* **Anatomical Junction:** Posterior Thoracic Erectors, Rhomboids, Suboccipitals, Contralateral Obliques.
* **Normal Function:** SBL and SPL posterior chain decelerates high-speed thoracic rotation after impact (`impact_to_hands_chest_height` to finish) to protect spinal facets.
* **Failure Mode (Post-Impact Collapse Switch):** Thoracic erectors fail to decelerate rotation smoothly $\rightarrow$ torso collapses into hyper-lordosis or abrupt spin-out.

## Vault Report Boundary & Clinical Rules

When referencing golf switch failure modes in app reports:
1. **Never report force/torque as measured:** Use *"Motion descriptors may be consistent with an X-Factor Dissociation Switch failure"*, never *"Measured 3D torso torque loss"*.
2. **Respect kinetic proxy non-upgrade rule:** Camera geometry describes image-plane motion; ground reaction moments at COP require force plates.
3. **Include retest prompt:** Cue step-drill or step-and-turn transition exercise.
