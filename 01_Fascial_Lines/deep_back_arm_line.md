# Deep Back Arm Line

## Definition

The Deep Back Arm Line is an Anatomy Trains pathway linking scapular and rotator-cuff structures to the posterior arm.

## Why It Matters

It provides the vault's deep posterior bridge from rib cage and scapula through the shoulder to the arm without asserting measured cuff activity or tissue loading.

## Stable Anatomy (Level 1 & 2)

The current node records [[rhomboids]], [[triceps_brachii]], [[supraspinatus]], [[infraspinatus]], [[teres_minor]], and [[subscapularis]], spanning the [[shoulder_joint]] and [[elbow_joint]] with proximal relationships to the [[thoracic_spine]] and scapula. Stable membership does not establish stabilisation, activation, or load in golf.

## Golf Application Interpretation (Level 3 & 4 context)

Kwon describes the external mechanics; the following line mapping is the vault's Anatomy Trains-based golf interpretation.

The bridge is rib cage/[[thoracic_spine]] -> scapula/[[rhomboids]] -> rotator-cuff region/[[shoulder_joint]] -> [[deep_back_arm_line]] -> elbow/hand. It connects contextually to the [[spiral_line]], [[back_functional_line]], and other Functional Lines. External mechanics do not establish cuff activation or force transfer through this route.

## App Hypotheses (Level 5)

Camera data may describe shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand, and scapular-region landmark trajectories where visible. It cannot measure rotator-cuff activation, force, joint moment, fascial tension, DBAL loading, or energy. Kinematics must not be reported as a diagnosis of cuff dysfunction or an instruction for treatment.
