# Kuljian (GMoney Movement): Hip Internal Rotation and Squat Context

## Source and role

Garrett Kuljian, [Hip Internal Rotation & Better Squats—Is There Really a Correlation?](https://www.gmoneymvmt.com/blog/hi), checked 2026-10-28. Applied clinical blog commentary, Level 4 `applied_practice`; not a diagnostic or instrumented validation study. The visible page gives November 3 without a verified year.

## Author's discussion

- “Strength” and “Squat Biomechanics”: distinguish maximum-strength goals from deep-squat mobility goals and discuss task-dependent stance options.
- “Hip Pain”: discusses pelvic orientation, sensitivity, and several possible contributors to restricted rotation. These are the author's clinical explanations, not findings measured by this app.
- “Flexibility/Mobility”: includes the author's personal experience of addressing superficial hip flexors/adductors before progressing hip rotation.
- “Table Tests vs Loaded Test”: passive examination and loaded performance provide different information; one does not determine the other.
- “Closing thoughts”: stance, heel elevation and counterweight are possible task modifications. No fixed hip-rotation prerequisite is established for learning to squat.

## Transfer to TillYes — Level 5 engine synthesis

Record task goals, entered symptoms, optional directly assessed mobility and actual setup separately from camera observations. A change with a wedge, counterweight or stance is a response to a changed task with competing explanations; it does not confirm hip clearance, passive ankle restriction or a fascial block. A positive table test is not proof of the cause of a video pattern.

The former 55–85% descent window, 70–90° block rule, pitch–yaw correlation cutoff and named middle-out/bottom-up classifiers are withdrawn: this source does not establish their validity.
