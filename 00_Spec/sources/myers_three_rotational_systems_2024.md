# Our Three Rotational Muscle Systems

Myers, T. W. (2024), *Our Three Rotational Muscle Systems*, Massage & Bodywork, May/June 2024. [ABMP Publication](https://www.abmp.com/Massage-and-Bodywork-Magazine/Issues/mayjune-2024/our-three-rotational-muscle-systems). See also Anatomy Trains course *The Arm Lines and the 3 Spirals with Tom Myers* (Myers, T. W.).

## Source Framework Overview

Thomas Myers organizes rotational coordination, elastic recoil, and multi-segmental control in bipedal locomotion (walking and running) across three concentric myofascial layers:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ OUTER SYSTEM: FUNCTIONAL LINES (BFL & FFL)                                  │
│ Superficial dynamic propulsion and cross-body contralateral power sling      │
│ (Latissimus Dorsi ⟷ Thoracolumbar Fascia ⟷ Gluteus Max ⟷ Vastus Lateralis)  │
└──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MIDDLE SYSTEM: THE SPIRAL LINE (SPL)                                        │
│ Torso-pelvis helical spring, lateral hip recoil, and stirrup stabilization   │
│ (Splenius Capitis ⟷ Rhomboids ⟷ Serratus ⟷ Obliques ⟷ TFL/ITB ⟷ Biceps Fem)  │
└──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ INNER SYSTEM: THE DEEP FRONT LINE (DFL)                                     │
│ Deep axial core pillar, sagittal/coronal centration, and pelvic levelness   │
│ (Tibialis Posterior ⟷ Popliteus ⟷ Adductor Magnus ⟷ Psoas ⟷ Diaphragm)       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Outer System — Functional Lines (FL) [Full Limb Propulsion]
- **Anatomy**:
  - **Back Functional Line (BFL)**: Latissimus dorsi $\rightarrow$ thoracolumbar fascia $\rightarrow$ contralateral gluteus maximus $\rightarrow$ lateral thigh via the **vastus lateralis down to the patellar tendon and tibial tuberosity**.
  - **Front Functional Line (FFL)**: Pectoralis major (lower fibers) $\rightarrow$ rectus abdominis sheath $\rightarrow$ contralateral pubic tubercle and **adductor longus**.
- **Role in Running**:
  - Dynamically couples contralateral arm swing with the opposite driving leg during late stance propulsion and toe-off.
  - Generates cross-body elastic momentum during rapid hip extension and trailing knee drive.

### 2. Middle System — The Spiral Line (SPL) [Lateral Spring & Stirrup]
- **Anatomy**:
  - Double-helix continuous loop originating at the occipital ridge and splenius capitis $\rightarrow$ rhomboids $\rightarrow$ serratus anterior $\rightarrow$ external oblique $\rightarrow$ abdominal aponeurosis $\rightarrow$ contralateral internal oblique $\rightarrow$ anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS).
  - Continues down the lateral thigh and leg: **Tensor Fasciae Latae (TFL) $\rightarrow$ Iliotibial Band (ITB) $\rightarrow$ Tibialis Anterior $\rightarrow$ passing under the foot arch via the Peroneal stirrup (Peroneus Longus) $\rightarrow$ ascending via Biceps Femoris (hamstrings) to the ischial tuberosity $\rightarrow$ sacrotuberous ligament $\rightarrow$ erector spinae** back to the cranium.
- **Role in Running**:
  - Coordinates torso-pelvis rotational dissociation and counter-rotation.
  - Acts as the primary **lateral elastic spring** recruited during stance-side shoulder tipping and hip hike (as observed in Kelvin Kiptum): stretching stance TFL/ITB and contralateral obliques during midstance compression and snapping back to propel the body into flight.
  - Stabilizes knee tracking relative to foot pronation/supination via the peroneal stirrup sling.

### 3. Inner System — Deep Front Line (DFL) [Deep Core Axis]
- **Anatomy**:
  - Originates deep in the sole of the foot: **Tibialis Posterior, Flexor Digitorum Longus, Flexor Hallucis Longus** $\rightarrow$ ascending behind the medial malleolus $\rightarrow$ crossing the **popliteus and posterior knee capsule** $\rightarrow$ ascending the medial thigh via **Adductor Magnus** $\rightarrow$ pelvic floor $\rightarrow$ **Psoas Major / Iliacus** $\rightarrow$ diaphragm $\rightarrow$ anterior longitudinal ligament, scalenes, and longus colli/capitis to the basilar occiput.
- **Role in Running**:
  - Acts as the single-leg vertical structural pillar, providing sagittal and coronal axial centration.
  - Mediates pelvic levelness, resists excessive pelvic tilt/drop during single-leg support, and maintains upright axial integrity without relying on brute superficial muscle co-contraction.

## Guiding the Investigation of Running Asymmetry

Myers' three systems organize clinical hypotheses when TillYes detects bilateral differences:
1. **Outer System Asymmetry**: If contralateral arm-to-leg swing phase timing or trailing knee push-off extension differs between left and right stance $\rightarrow$ investigate contralateral Functional Line load transfer and arm-drive coordination.
2. **Middle System Asymmetry**: If shoulder-line tilt and pelvic-line tilt display asymmetric excursion, altered phase lag, or unilateral hip drop $\rightarrow$ investigate Spiral Line helical recoil, TFL/ITB lateral spring loading, and foot stirrup control.
3. **Inner System Asymmetry**: If single-leg stance shows uncompensated pelvic drop, excessive lateral pelvic translation, or medial arch collapse without coordinated trunk counter-rotation $\rightarrow$ investigate Deep Front Line axial centration, psoas function, and tibialis posterior support.

## App Boundary and Guardrails

- **Level 5 Hypothesis Layer**: Myers' fascial lines provide a qualitative anatomical and conceptual framework for clinical reasoning. They are **not** direct tissue measurements.
- Camera 2D landmarks record image-plane angles, offsets, and cycle timing; they cannot measure fascial tensile stress in Pascals, tissue stiffness, or EMG muscle recruitment.
- Permitted reporting language: "May warrant clinician investigation of middle-system or spiral line rotational coordination."
- Forbidden claims: "Spiral line is tight," "Deep front line failure detected," "Adductor weakness diagnosed."
