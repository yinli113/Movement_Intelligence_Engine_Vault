# Leardini et al. (2007): Multi-Segment Foot Kinematics During Stance

*Gait & Posture*. 2007;25(3):453–462. [PubMed (16839757)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16839757/). Reviewed online 2026-11-13; no local PDF archive claimed.

## Direct evidence

Instrumented 3D multi-segment foot kinematic study (Rizzoli Foot Model) treating shank, calcaneus (rearfoot), midfoot, and metatarsus (forefoot) as separate 3D rigid segments during the stance phase of walking. Demonstrated consistent coronal-plane rearfoot eversion/inversion, midfoot deformation, and forefoot motion profiles that align with the foot's dual biomechanical role: acting as a flexible shock absorber (pronation / rearfoot eversion during early stance) and transitioning into a rigid propulsive lever (re-supination / rearfoot inversion in late stance).

## Transfer boundary

Supports describing **subtalar/rearfoot pronation (eversion) in early stance** and **re-supination (inversion) in mid-to-late stance** as instrumented 3D biomechanical context, and proves that a single rigid-foot model cannot measure intra-foot or triplanar motion. A 2D single-camera app **cannot** measure triplanar subtalar pronation/supination from a side view; at best, posterior/anterior views can observe coarse frontal-plane rearfoot alignment or medial-arch drop as non-diagnostic descriptors.
