# Non-structural misalignments of body posture in the sagittal plane

## Source Role

This source is a **Level 1 foundational framework** for the static-posture domain and the engine's canonical **clinical-philosophy** source. It defines the non-structural sagittal-posture taxonomy and the principle that visible posture or reduced flexibility does not prove true muscle-fibre shortening — hypoactivity, hyperactivity, compensation, and the structural-versus-non-structural distinction must also be considered. This is the source behind the app's guiding question ("How does your body organise movement?" rather than "What posture do you have?").

It is **not** a gold-standard biomechanics reference and **not** a replacement in depth for Kendall et al. *Muscles: Testing and Function with Posture and Pain* or Sahrmann *Diagnosis and Treatment of Movement Impairment Syndromes* — those are recorded as **future domain-expansion sources**, not required blockers for the MVP. A foundational clinical philosophy does not, by itself, authorise any measurement, causal interpretation, or muscle-by-muscle treatment conclusion; claims must be traced to this source and `source_role: foundational_clinical_philosophy` only for the philosophy, taxonomy, and cautious-interpretation rules they support. Use [[anatomy_trains_myofascial_thomas_w_myers]] / [[bodyreading_static_posture]] for the fascial-line reading layer.

## App Philosophy (canonical statement)

The app's guiding question, per this source, is **not** "What posture do you have?" but **"How does your body organize movement?"** Operationally, that means:

- A static posture is read as a **pattern hypothesis** (as in BodyReading), AND
- The muscles involved are read by **functional organization** — which stabilizers are hypoactive (under-recruited, often lengthened-but-weak) and which mobilizers are hyperactive (over-recruited, often shortened-but-inflexible) — not by a naive "shortened = strong, lengthened = weak" rule.
- The deficit of locomotor stability triggers a compensatory mechanism: the stabilizing function is overtaken by mobilizers, producing hyperactivity and decreased flexibility, which may lead to a pathological chain within the musculoskeletal system (local PDF p.2).
- Therefore corrective logic targets **regaining stabilizer activity** (e.g., gluteus maximus) rather than blindly stretching or strengthening the lengthened/shortened muscle (local PDF p.12-13, the hamstrings example).

This philosophy is the reason the vault separates a posture *label* (Level 2 pattern hypothesis) from a *functional-organization* interpretation (Level 2 functional model) and from any *diagnosis* (forbidden without clinical assessment).

## Relevant Sections

- Background — human body posture definition, Kendall et al. definition of good posture, sagittal/coronal/transverse planes, the optimal head-line/base-line overlap.
- Non-structural vs structural misalignments — differential diagnosis; flexibility testing distinguishes non-structural (corrects on active extension) from structural (e.g., Scheuermann) (Figs. 2-5).
- Functional muscle classification by Bergmark and Richardson — local (mono-articular) stabilizers vs global (multi-articular) stabilizers vs mobilizers; reflectory inhibition (hypoactivity) vs reflectory excitation (hyperactivity).
- Muscle groups maintaining good body posture — the local stabilizer list (multifidus, transversus abdominis, diaphragm, pelvic floor, etc.) and the global stabilizer/mobilizer lists.
- Lordotic / Kyphotic / Kyphotic-lordotic / Flat-back / Sway-back postures — each with a body-part position table and a lengthened/shortened/hypoactive/hyperactive muscle table.
- Discussion — clinical relevance for corrective exercise; the hamstrings example showing lengthened does not imply weak, and shortening is not the same as contracture; corrective exercise should reduce mobilizer activity and regain stabilizer activity, working in internal (not full) range of motion.
- Limitations — sagittal plane only; does not cover psychosocial, nutritional, structural, or fascial-system factors.

## Source Summary for Graph Use

Czaprowski et al.'s central contribution is a **sagittal posture taxonomy** plus a **functional muscle-organization model** that together reframe posture from a static label to a movement-organization question. For the vault's static-posture engine, the useful extraction is:

- Four (+1 combined) sagittal posture types, each defined by body-part positions (head, cervical/thoracic/lumbar spine, pelvis, hip, knee, ankle) and by which muscles are lengthened/shortened and hypoactive/hyperactive.
- The Bergmark/Richardson [[stabilizer_mobilizer_classification]]: local stabilizers (deep, mono-articular, joint-segment control, prone to reflectory inhibition/hypoactivity), global stabilizers (antigravity, multi-articular), and mobilizers (movement-producing, prone to reflectory excitation/hyperactivity and decreased flexibility).
- The key clinical rule: **muscle lengthening does not equal weakness, and muscle shortening does not equal contracture** — flexibility tests (popliteal angle, long-sitting) and posture type can disagree, and the resolution is the functional-organization reading, not a length-only rule (local PDF p.12-13).
- Corrective exercise targets restoring stabilizer activity and reducing mobilizer overactivity, performed in internal range of motion, not naive stretching/strengthening of the apparently short/long muscle.

## Domain Interpretation

This source supports static-posture pattern hypotheses and the functional-organization layer of the app:

- [[bodyreading_static_posture]] (fascial-line reading from Anatomy Trains) + [[stabilizer_mobilizer_classification]] (functional reading from this source) = a two-layer posture interpretation: which fascial line appears shortened AND which stabilizer is under-recruited / which mobilizer is over-recruited.
- A [[lordotic_posture]] reading is a hypothesis: increased lumbar lordosis + anterior pelvic tilt, with gluteus maximus hypoactive and hamstrings hyperactive (compensating), hip flexors shortened. The app reports this as an organization pattern, not a diagnosis.
- The structural-vs-non-structural distinction (flexibility testing) governs whether a posture finding is reportable as a correctable pattern (non-structural) or must be flagged for clinical referral (structural).
