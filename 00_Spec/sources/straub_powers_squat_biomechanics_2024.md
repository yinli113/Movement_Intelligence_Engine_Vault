# Straub and Powers Squat Biomechanics (2024)

## Source Role

This clinical commentary organizes squat biomechanics around modifiable technique
parameters: trunk inclination, tibia inclination, foot rotation, stance width, and
depth. It is the applied-practice anchor for the first bodyweight-squat app, not a
diagnostic-validation study or a universal definition of normal squat technique.

The article reports its own Level of Evidence as 5. Within the vault's different
five-level hierarchy it is Level 4 because it is an applied clinical commentary.
The instrumented studies it cites must be reviewed separately before their findings
are promoted into Level 3 canonical thresholds.

## Directly Supported Claims

- Trunk and tibia inclinations interact when characterizing relative hip-versus-knee
  extensor demand; either angle interpreted alone can be misleading.
- The article describes trunk-tibia angle greater than 10 degrees as relatively
  hip-extensor biased, between -10 and 10 degrees as neutral, and less than -10
  degrees as relatively knee-extensor biased.
- Squat depth, stance width, and foot rotation alter biomechanical demand and must be
  recorded rather than treated as irrelevant setup variation.
- The article operationally groups depth as shallow (0-90 degrees knee flexion),
  medium (90-110 degrees or thigh approximately parallel), and deep (110-135 degrees).
- Condition-specific recommendations are exercise-prescription considerations for
  people with established clinical conditions; they are not rules for diagnosing a
  condition from squat appearance.

## App Translation

- A calibrated side-view video may calculate 2D trunk inclination, tibia inclination,
  their difference, and a knee-flexion depth proxy.
- These are image-plane kinematic proxies. They do not measure joint moment, muscle
  activation, tissue load, pathology, pain source, or fascial-line loading.
- Every finding must record stance, foot position, arm position, selected depth,
  view quality, and algorithm version.

## Source Limitations

- Clinical commentary rather than a diagnostic or markerless-validation study.
- Synthesizes studies with differing loads, squat styles, populations, and methods.
- Several muscle-activation findings are inconsistent across the reviewed studies.
- Clinical-condition sections cannot be reversed into pathology inference.
