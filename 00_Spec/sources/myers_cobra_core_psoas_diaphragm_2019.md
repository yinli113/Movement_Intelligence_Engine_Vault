# The Cobra at Your Core: The Psoas-Diaphragm Connection

## Source Role

This source is a **Level 1 foundational anatomical and clinical framework** for the Deep Front Line core and static sagittal posture analysis. It bridges Thomas Myers' whole-body [[deep_front_line]] model with granular clinical palpation, movement assessment, and sagittal posture reading.

It establishes that:
1. The **psoas major** and **respiratory diaphragm** form a continuous anatomical myofascial unit ("the cobra") connected intimately at the diaphragmatic crura and medial/lateral arcuate ligaments in front of T12–L2.
2. In sagittal posture analysis, the psoas-diaphragm core presents two common non-functional departures from vertical alignment:
   - **Depressed Cobra (Image 4C):** The psoas loses resting tone, the upper lumbar spine falls back into posterior thoracolumbar sag / sway-back, and the diaphragmatic dome falls in front. The [[superficial_back_line]] (suboccipitals and thoracic extensors) and posterior guy-wires ([[quadratus_lumborum]] and thoracolumbar fascia) are loaded under continuous tension, while the head projects forward to keep the visual horizon horizontal.
   - **Over-Enthusiastic Cobra (Image 4D):** The psoas is hypertonic / locked-short, pulling the upper lumbars forward into anterior lordosis and tilting the diaphragm back.
3. **Clinical Handling Precaution (Myers' Rule):** In a Depressed Cobra presentation, the psoas is lengthened and exhausted (locked-long), NOT locked-short. Aggressive stripping or poking of the psoas is contraindicated; practitioners must free the posterior guy-wires (QL and TLF), mobilize the diaphragm, and tone/revivify the psoas with gentle movement (e.g. water wading, hip flexion with extended knee).
