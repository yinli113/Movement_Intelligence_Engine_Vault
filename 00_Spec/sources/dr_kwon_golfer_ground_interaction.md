# Dr. Young-Hoo Kwon: Golfer-Ground Interaction Moments

## Definition

This source details the biomechanical principles governing the golfer's interaction with the ground, specifically the generation of Ground Reaction Forces (GRFs) and Ground Reaction Moments (GRMs or FGMOM) about the golfer's Center of Mass (COM).

Url: [drkwongolf.info/biom/fgmom.html (Dr. Kwon Golf Biomechanics)](http://drkwongolf.info/biom/fgmom.html#:~:text=One%20thing%20clearly%20shown%20in,contributes%20to%20the%20clubhead%20speed.)

## Why It Matters

Dr. Kwon's work mathematically bridges the gap between ground force generation and body rotation. In golf, clubhead speed is not generated purely by muscular torque in the torso and arms; it is driven from the ground up by converting Ground Reaction Forces into external torque (moments) about the body's Center of Mass. Pushing down and against the ground generates the necessary forces to rotate the pelvis, transfer energy through the core, and accelerate the club.

## Supporting Evidence

Dr. Kwon provides a mathematical and visual breakdown of three classes of golfer-ground interaction moments:
1. **GRF Moments**: The moment created by the net Ground Reaction Force vectors acting with a moment arm relative to the Center of Mass (COM).
2. **Pivoting Moments**: The local moments generated by individual feet spinning against the ground (horizontal friction moments).
3. **Foot Contact Moments**: The local torque between the foot sole and the ground (restricting rocking or tilting).

A key excerpt addresses a common golf coaching misconception regarding vertical forces:
> *"One thing clearly shown in Figure 2 is that even a vertical GRF [Ground Reaction Force] can generate a moment about the COM [Center of Mass] as long as the force vector does not pass through the COM. In other words, **pushing the ground down hard can affect the rotational motion of the body/club and thus contributes to the clubhead speed.**"*
