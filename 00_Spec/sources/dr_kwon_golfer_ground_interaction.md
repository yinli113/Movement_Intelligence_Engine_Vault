# Dr. Young-Hoo Kwon: Golfer-Ground Interaction Research Program

## Definition

This dossier is the canonical Level 3 register for Dr. Young-Hoo Kwon's golf-biomechanics descriptions of golfer-ground interaction, swing events and phases, kinematics and kinetics, orientation angles, and the functional swing plane. It anchors external-force and external-moment terminology without treating those measurements as evidence of fascial loading or as quantities recoverable from ordinary video.

## Terminology

| Term | Evidence-safe meaning in this dossier |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ground reaction force (GRF) | The net reaction force supplied by the ground to a foot; a vector with magnitude, direction, and point of action. |
| Ground reaction moment (GRM) | The net moment remaining from distributed foot-ground reactions when represented with a GRF at the COP; it is not the same quantity as GRF. |
| Center of pressure (COP) | The point of action of a GRF. Combined COP reflects the relative vertical-GRF magnitudes under the feet and is not the body's COM. |
| Center of mass (COM) | The mass-weighted location used as the reference point for whole-body linear motion and, for a freely moving body, for assessing the angular effect of external force. |
| Moment of force / torque | A vector describing the angular effect of a force about a reference point; magnitude depends on force and perpendicular moment arm. |
| Kinematics | Description and measurement of motion: time, position, displacement, velocity, acceleration, trajectories, and orientations. |
| Kinetics | Explanation of motion through forces and moments; joint kinetics require kinetic measurements and modelling such as inverse dynamics. |
| Functional swing plane (FSP) | Kwon et al.'s mathematically fitted plane based on the clubhead trajectory from mid-downswing (MD) to mid-follow-through (MF), not an assertion that the whole swing is planar. |

## Claim-Level Evidence

| GRF versus GRM | GRF is the net force; GRM is the net moment of the distributed reaction forces represented at COP. They are mechanically distinct vector quantities. | `kwon_grf_grm`, Figures 1 and 3 and accompanying definitions | A force magnitude does not disclose a moment without point-of-action/moment-arm information. |
| COP versus COM | COP is the GRF point of action; COM is the body's mass-weighted location and a reference for whole-body motion. | `kwon_grf_grm`, Figure 4 paragraph; `kwon_moment`, Figure 3 | COP is not a video-visible proxy for COM and neither should be substituted for the other. |
| COP shift versus weight shift | Kwon states that combined COP location reflects relative vertical forces under the two feet and “has nothing to do with” body COM position; COP shift and weight shift should not be used synonymously. | `kwon_grf_grm`, Figure 4 and following paragraph | This is a terminology boundary, not permission to infer vertical forces from apparent lateral body motion. |
| Frontal-plane/F-B-axis GRF moment about COM | The combined/resultant GRF acting through combined COP contributes a moment about golfer COM; the Kwon component is the cross product $\mathbf r_{COM\rightarrow combined\ COP}\times\mathbf F_{combined}$ projected to the F/B axis. | `kwon_fgmom`, “GRF Moments,” Figures 2–4; `kwon_moment`, Figures 3–5 | The moment cannot be calculated from posture alone because the measured GRF vector and 3-D geometry are required. |
| Pivoting moment | The vertical-axis pivoting moment is generated by individual horizontal foot GRFs about the vertical axis through combined COP; Kwon identifies it as the primary golfer-ground interaction moment about that axis. | `kwon_fgmom`, “Pivoting Moments,” Figure 5; `han_etal_2019_ground_interaction`, abstract | It is not a visual label for pelvis rotation and is not identical to a foot-contact GRM. Do not add it to a full foot-by-foot COM moment sum, whose vertical-axis part already contains the separated-foot force couple. |
| Foot-contact moment | Foot-contact moments are direct/residual torsional GRMs acting at the foot-ground interfaces and were the smallest of the three reported axis-specific interaction-moment components in the skilled-male sample. | `kwon_fgmom`, “Foot Contact Moments,” Figure 6; `han_etal_2019_ground_interaction`, abstract/method | Sample findings should not be universalized to every golfer, surface, footwear, or task. |
| Force magnitude versus direction | Kwon's model treats magnitude, direction, and point of action as jointly important; large GRF magnitude alone does not establish mechanically effective interaction. | `kwon_fgmom`, “Moments Acting on the Body”; `kwon_moment`, Figures 1–5 | A magnitude-only or posture-only observation cannot establish the external moment. |
| Pressure-mat versus force-plate capability | A pressure mat reports COP and vertical-force information; a force plate reports three-component GRF/GRM and COP, while two force plates permit foot-specific interaction-moment calculations. | `kwon_grf_grm`, Figures 3–4 and instrument comparison | Pressure data are not full 3-D force/moment data. Ordinary video supplies neither. |
| Event/phase timing | Kwon defines research events from BA through LF using club or body criteria and distinguishes practical BS/DS/FT phases from an extended downswing beginning at EPR. | `kwon_events_phases`, “Swing Events” and “Swing Phases,” Figures 1–3 | Event definitions are operational conventions; source-defined timing must remain separate from vault phases or coaching labels. |
| Kinematics versus kinetics | Motion positions, angles, velocities, accelerations, paths, and event timing are kinematic; forces and moments explaining motion are kinetic. | `kwon_kinematics_kinetics`, opening definitions and joint-motion paragraphs | Ordinary video can support qualified kinematic observations, but not direct kinetic measurement. |
| Orientation-angle sequence | Pelvis and thorax orientation angles depend on the selected consecutive rotation sequence. | `kwon_orientation_angles`, opening definition and Figure 1 | Angle labels are not comparable unless coordinate frames and rotation sequence are specified. |
| Functional swing plane | The FSP is fitted from the MD–MF clubhead trajectory; the 2012 study describes skilled golfers as semi-planar rather than globally planar. | `kwon_functional_swing_plane`, Figures 1–2; `kwon_etal_2012_planarity`, abstract | FSP is a kinematic construct, not evidence of force, tissue loading, or a single anatomical plane. |
| Golfer-ground parameters and clubhead speed | In 63 highly skilled male golfers measured with optical motion capture and two force plates, selected peak force and moment parameters were significantly associated with maximum clubhead speed. | `han_etal_2019_ground_interaction`, abstract | Association is not proof that increasing any isolated peak will cause greater speed in an individual. |
| Torsional separation and sequence | The 2019 study compared skilled-male groups defined by pelvis-shoulders separation style and reported variation in downswing angular-velocity peak sequences. | `han_etal_2019_torsional_separation`, abstract | The paper supports group-specific kinematic findings, not a universal ideal sequence or fascial mechanism. |

## What Kwon Directly Supports

- An external-force and external-moment account of golfer-ground interaction using measured GRFs, GRMs, COP, COM-referenced moment arms, and defined non-overlapping axis-specific moment components.
- Operational definitions for golf-swing events and phases, including distinctions among TB, EPR, BI, MF, and an extended downswing.
- The separation of kinematic descriptions from kinetic explanations and the need for appropriate measurement and modelling for each.
- Peer-reviewed, sample-bounded findings about skilled male golfers, golfer-ground parameters, torsional-separation styles, kinematic sequences, and functional swing-plane planarity.

## What Kwon Does Not Directly Support

- Kwon does not directly establish fascia, myofascial-line loading, myofascial elasticity, or tissue-specific force transmission.
- Kwon does not validate MediaPipe scores, landmark-derived kinetic scores, or app thresholds.
- Kwon does not permit force, pressure, torque, impulse, energy, muscle activation, or fascial tension to be inferred from ordinary monocular or multicamera video without the corresponding instruments and validated model.
- Kwon does not show that visible COP-like motion is measured COP, that pelvis motion is a pivoting moment, or that COM position is equivalent to pressure distribution.
- Kwon does not establish that a group association, timing pattern, or model component is a universal causal prescription for an individual golfer.

Kwon supports the external-force and external-moment description of golfer-ground interaction (Level 3). Anatomy Trains supplies the separate structural line model (Level 1). Mapping between those layers is a vault golf interpretation and is not a direct finding of Kwon's research.

## Evidence Level

**Level 3 — Golf Biomechanics Research.** The peer-reviewed papers and Kwon's primary biomechanics pages can validate golf-specific kinematics, kinetics, event definitions, and instrumented golfer-ground interaction. They do not replace Level 1 fascial anatomy, Level 2 general-biomechanics evidence, Level 4 coaching interpretation, or Level 5 app hypotheses.

## App Use

- Use source-defined swing events as labelled anchors only when the required body/club landmarks and camera conditions make the event observable; record any vault remapping separately.
- Use kinematic constructs for video-derived descriptions with calibration, uncertainty, and visibility limits stated.
- Reserve GRF, GRM, COP, pressure, torque, impulse, energy, muscle activation, and fascial tension for measured or otherwise independently validated data. Do not output them as direct findings from ordinary video.
- Any MediaPipe metric, score, threshold, or report phrase is Level 5 app logic and must be labelled as a hypothesis rather than attributed to Kwon.
