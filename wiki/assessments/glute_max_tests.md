# Test Protocol: Gluteus Maximus Strength & Stretch Tests

Standardized diagnostic protocols to evaluate the functional capacity and tissue length of the Gluteus Maximus.

---

## 1. Strength Test: Single-Leg Bridge Test (Endurance & Symmetry)
- **Objective**: Assess the endurance and firing symmetry of the glute max.
- **Protocol**:
  1. Have the client lie supine with knees bent at $90^\circ$, feet flat.
  2. Lift one foot off the ground and extend the leg.
  3. Lift hips off the floor into a single-leg bridge, aligning the thigh, pelvis, and torso.
  4. Hold this position for time.
- **Fail Criteria**:
  - Pelvis drops or rotates (poor pelvic stability).
  - Cramping in the hamstrings (indicates hamstring dominance and glute amnesia).
  - Lower back arches excessively (lumbar spine compensating for hip extension).
- **Target**: Hold for 40 seconds symmetrically on both sides with zero hamstring cramping.

## 2. Stretch Test: Supine Knee-To-Chest (Glute Length Test)
- **Objective**: Test the length (flexibility) of the gluteus maximus fibers.
- **Protocol**:
  1. Lie flat on your back, legs straight.
  2. Pull one knee up toward your chest, keeping the opposite leg flat on the floor (do not let it bend or lift).
- **Measurement**:
  - The knee should easily reach the chest (approx. $110^\circ - 120^\circ$ of hip flexion) without the opposite leg lifting or the knee flaring out laterally.
- **Fail Criteria**:
  - If the thigh flares out (abducts) while pulling to the chest, it indicates restriction in the gluteus maximus and posterior joint capsule.
